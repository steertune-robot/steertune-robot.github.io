Abstract

Steertuning adapts robots to new tasks and environments from just 5 human demonstrations. A 400M-parameter diffusion policy, pretrained on roughly 100 hours of egocentric human interactions, combines two-minute LoRA finetuning with demonstration-guided action steering.

Trained entirely on human data, the policy transfers to a mobile manipulator and achieves 71% success across 12 tasks in 12 new environments. The full process—from collecting demonstrations to robot deployment—takes under five minutes.

Overview

From egocentric human data to a YOR mobile manipulator with Wuji hands: pretrain, finetune, and steer.

Key contributions

  1. Rapid task adaptation. Five demonstrations; under five minutes.
  2. Human-only policy learning. Human video supervises pretraining and adaptation.
  3. End-to-end mobile manipulation. Language connects navigation and learned manipulation.

Method

Egocentric human data

Aria Gen 2 recordings yield 109.6 hours, 3.69M frames, and 48.7k generated instructions. Contact segmentation and object grounding create training snippets; removing body geometry helps transfer human observations to the robot.

Policy architecture and action steering

A diffusion transformer takes point clouds, object masks, fingertip locations, and language as inputs. It predicts human action landmarks, retargeted to the robot at deployment.

Low-rank finetuning

Freeze the pretrained model; train rank-8 LoRA adapters for 2,000 updates—about two minutes.

Demonstration-guided steering

Match a demonstration by object layout, noise its actions, and blend with model predictions at w = 0.3.

xt ← w a⋆t + (1 − w) xt(2)

Discussion and Limitations

Human video lacks contact measurements, limiting dexterity. Kinematic retargeting can also fail, even when predicted human actions are accurate.
